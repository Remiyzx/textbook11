Grammar: Clock Time vs Duration
1. When to use "diǎnzhōng" and "fēnzhōng":
diǎn / diǎnzhōng (Clock Time)
"diǎnzhōng" is exclusive to FULL hours. NEVER used with minutes. liǎng diǎn(zhōng) (2:00)
✘ liǎng diǎnzhōng shí fēn
fēn / fēnzhōng (Duration)
"fēnzhōng" is for a period of time. Don't use it to tell the exact time on a clock. hái yǒu shí fēnzhōng (still have 10 minutes)
✘ liǎng diǎn shí fēnzhōng
2. Reading the Clock (Less than 10 mins & Quarters):
  • 2:05 ➔ liǎng diǎn líng wǔ fēn (Less than 10, add 'líng')
  • 2:10 ➔ liǎng diǎn shí fēn
  • 2:12 ➔ liǎng diǎn shí'èr (fēn) (More than 10, 'fēn' may be omitted)
  • 2:15 ➔ liǎng diǎn shíwǔ (fēn) OR liǎng diǎn yí kè
  • 2:30 ➔ liǎng diǎn sānshí (fēn) OR liǎng diǎn bàn
  • 2:45 ➔ liǎng diǎn sìshíwǔ (fēn) OR chà yí kè sān diǎn
  • 2:55 ➔ liǎng diǎn wǔshíwǔ (fēn) OR chà wǔ fēn sān diǎn
Grammar & Culture: Asking Age Appropriately

In Chinese culture, how you ask someone's age depends entirely on who you are speaking to:

Infants (<1 year)
Bǎobao jǐ ge yuè le?
Chinese counts age in months for infants (How many months old?)
Children (<12 years)
Nǐ jīnnián jǐ suì?
Sounds natural/cute for kids. Weird for adults.
Adults (Peers)
Nǐ jīnnián duō dà?
Most common & natural. Not too formal/strange.
Elderly (60+ years)
Nín jīnnián duō dà suìshu?
Extra polite. Shows respect.
⚠️ Warning: Nǐ duōshao suì?

Naturally carries a cold, statistical vibe. It sounds like a police officer asking: "State your age." Avoid using this in conversation!

Grammar: The Difference Between kěyǐ, huì, and néng
kěyǐ (boleh) huì (pandai) néng (dapat)
Permission / Appropriateness
  • Asking or giving permission; allowed.
  • When conditions make it suitable.
• Wǒ kěyǐ qù yóuyǒng ma?
(Can I go swimming?)

• Zhèr bù kěyǐ yóuyǒng.
(You can't swim here. Forbidden!)
Allowed or not
 Learned Skill / Ability
  • Talking about skills you have learned (swimming, languages, etc.).
  • No ability because never learned.
• Wǒ huì yóuyǒng.
(I can swim. I learned it.)

• Wǒ bú huì yóuyǒng.
(I can't swim. I never learned it.)
Learned or not
 Physical / Conditional Ability
  • Talking about strength or conditions that allow something.
  • When external factors prevent action.
• Wǒ néng yóu shí gōnglǐ.
(I can swim 10km. I'm strong enough.)

• Wǒ bù néng yóuyǒng.
(I can't swim today. I'm sick.)
Physically possible or not
Grammar: Adverbs of Degree (hěn, zhēn, tài...le, bú tài)
bú tài (not very)
Objectively describes insufficiency. Weak.
Zhège píngguǒ bú tài dà.
hěn (very)
States facts/mild emphasis. Grammatically required before adjectives.
Zhège píngguǒ hěn dà.
zhēn (really)
Subjective feelings (direct observation). Shows surprise/liking NOW.
Zhège píngguǒ zhēn dà.
tài...le (too / extremely)
Extremely subjective evaluation. Exaggeration or complaints.
Zhège píngguǒ tài dà le.
Culture Note: Responding to Compliments
🙏
nǎli (Where? -> Oh, no, not really!)

When used as a response to a compliment, it expresses humility. Using "nǎli" or "shì ma" (Is that so?) shows politeness and avoids arrogance when receiving praise. This is considered very good manners in Chinese culture.

A: Nín de Hànyǔ hěn hǎo. (Your Chinese is very good.)

B: Nǎli, wǒ de Hànyǔ bú tài hǎo. (Not really, my Chinese is not very good.)

Text 1: Asking the Time (Pg 174–175)

Text 1: Dialogue Lines 1–19
1. (Uncle/Driver, I am going to the Language Institute. May I ask, what time is it now?)
Lín Nà: Shīfu, wǒ qù Yǔyán Xuéyuàn. Qǐngwèn xiànzài jǐ diǎn?
2. (It is a quarter to eight. You can speak Chinese!)
Sījī: Chà yí kè bā diǎn. Nín huì shuō Hànyǔ a!
3. (I can speak a little Chinese. I am a student, now I am returning to the institute to attend class.)
Lín Nà: Wǒ huì shuō yìdiǎnr Hànyǔ. Wǒ shì xuésheng, xiànzài huí xuéyuàn shàngkè.
4. (What time do you have class?)
Sījī: Nǐmen jǐ diǎn shàngkè?
5. (Class is at eight o'clock. Uncle/Driver, can we arrive at eight?)
Lín Nà: Bā diǎn shàngkè. Shīfu, wǒmen bā diǎn néng dào ma?
6. (We can arrive. Your Chinese is very good.)
Sījī: Néng dào. Nín de Hànyǔ hěn hǎo.
7. (Not really, my Chinese is not very good. Can you speak English?)
Lín Nà: Nǎli, wǒ de Hànyǔ bú tài hǎo. Nín huì bu huì shuō Yīngyǔ?
8. (I cannot speak English. I also like foreign languages, and often study a little English at home.)
Sījī: Wǒ bú huì shuō Yīngyǔ. Wǒ yě xǐhuan wàiyǔ, chángcháng zài jiā xué diǎnr Yīngyǔ.
9. (Who teaches you English?)
Lín Nà: Shéi jiāo nín Yīngyǔ?
10. (My granddaughter.)
Sījī: Wǒ sūnnür.
11. (Really interesting. How old is she this year?)
Lín Nà: Zhēn yǒu yìsi. Tā jīnnián jǐ suì?
12. (Six years old. My age is too big, learning English is not easy.)
Sījī: Liù suì. Wǒ de suìshu tài dà le, xué Yīngyǔ bù róngyì.
13. (How old are you this year?)
Lín Nà: Nín jīnnián duō dà suìshu?
14. (I am 52 this year. The Language Institute has arrived. Now it is five minutes to eight, you still have five minutes.)
Sījī: Wǒ jīnnián wǔshí'èr. Yǔyán Xuéyuàn dào le. Xiànzài chà wǔ fēn bā diǎn, nín hái yǒu wǔ fēnzhōng.
15. (Thank you, here is the money.)
Lín Nà: Xièxie, gěi nín qián.
16. (You gave me RM 20, I give you RM 5.40 change, OK?)
Sījī: Nín gěi wǒ èrshí, wǒ zhǎo nín wǔ kuài sì, OK?
17. (You can speak English!)
Lín Nà: Nín huì shuō Yīngyǔ!
18. (I can also speak a little. Bye-bye!)
Sījī: Wǒ yě huì yìdiǎnr. Báibái!
19. (Bye-bye!)
Lín Nà: Báibái!

Grammar: Adverb "hái" (Still / Yet)
1. "hái" meaning "also / in addition" (Review):
Lists extra information (fact A + hái + fact B) or asks for more (hái yào + something).
• Wǒ yǒu yí ge gēge, hái yǒu liǎng ge mèimei. (I have one older brother, and also two younger sisters.)
• Nín hái yào shénme? (Anything else you want?)
2. NEW Usage: "hái" meaning "Still / Yet":
Shows an action or state hasn't happened or changed yet. ALWAYS used with negative words "méi" or "bù".

+
Action
• Tā xiànzài hái méiyǒu qǐchuáng. (He still hasn't gotten up.)
• Wǒ hái bù huì shuō Yīngyǔ. (I still cannot speak English.)

Grammar: Double Objects Verbs ("jiāo" & "wèn")

Some verbs like jiāo (teach) and wèn (ask) can take TWO objects. The first object is always the PERSON, and the second is the THING/SUBJECT.

Text 2: Permission & Prohibition (Pg 177–178)

Text 2: Dialogue Lines 1–7
1. (Teacher Chen, Ma Dawei cannot come to class today.)
Dīng Lìbō: Chén lǎoshī, Mǎ Dàwéi jīntiān bù néng lái shàngkè.
2. (Why can't he come to class?)
Chén lǎoshī: Tā wèi shénme bù néng lái shàngkè?
3. (Yesterday was Sunday. In the morning he went to the shopping mall to buy things, in the afternoon he went to a friend's home to hang out. He returned to the institute at 11:30 p.m., at 12:00 a.m. he wrote Chinese characters, and at 2:00 a.m. he went to sleep. Now he still has not gotten up.)
Dīng Lìbō: Zuótiān shì xīngqīrì, tā shàngwǔ qù shāngchǎng mǎi dōngxi, xiàwǔ qù péngyou jiā wánr. Tā wǎnshang shíyī diǎn bàn huí xuéyuàn, shí'èr diǎn xiě Hànzì, liǎng diǎnzhōng shuìjiào. Xiànzài tā hái méiyǒu qǐchuáng.
4. (He should come to class.)
Chén lǎoshī: Tā yīnggāi lái shàngkè.
5. (Teacher, may I ask you a question?)
Dīng Lìbō: Lǎoshī, wǒ néng bu néng wèn nín yí ge wèntí?
6. (You may.)
Chén lǎoshī: Kěyǐ.
7. (Why do we have class at 8:00?)
Dīng Lìbō: Wǒmen wèi shénme bā diǎn shàngkè?
